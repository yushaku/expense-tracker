Eleven @Model types backed by SwiftData. Reviewed bank-statement import

A local, owner-confirmed sequence. Parsing and matching only prepare a review; no financial model changes until every row has a resolution and the owner confirms the summary.

SHA-256 stable file and row provenance atomic one financial save or rollback local PDF bytes never leave the device Scroll horizontally to follow the full sequence

  1. Share one PDF

    The owner exports a TPBank statement from a bank app or Files and shares exactly one PDF to MonMon.

    external file → Share Extension

  2. Validate and stage

    The extension checks the PDF signature and 25 MB limit, sanitizes its name, fingerprints its bytes, then atomically publishes a ready item.

    StatementIntakeStore · content-addressed

  3. Parse off-main

    The host app validates the manifest again, loads the bytes, and uses PDFKit to extract TPBank metadata, totals and normalized candidates.

    StatementImportInboxService → TPBankPDFStatementParser

  4. Reconcile current data

    Stable candidate fingerprints find exact imports first; amount, day, account and kind then surface possible transaction or transfer matches.

    StatementImportReconciler · no writes

  5. Owner resolves rows

    Each row becomes a new transaction, a new transfer, a link to an existing record, a skip, or already imported. Unresolved rows block commit.

    StatementImportReview · account + category checks

  6. Recheck, save, clean up

    A fresh context repeats reconciliation to reject stale reviews, saves all financial changes once, remembers the bank-account mapping, then removes the staged PDF.

    CommitService → SwiftData → UserDefaults → inbox cleanup

No referential integrity

No @Relationship, no cascade rules, no @Attribute(.unique). The store will happily keep a MoneyTransaction.accountID pointing at a deleted account. Editor guards stop an account deletion while any cash-moving record names it; deleting a holding or debt removes its dependent sales or payments in the same save. Instrument deletion deliberately leaves its positions visible but unpriced rather than deleting the owner's history.

A rule is not money

RecurringRule holds no balance and reaches no total. It stamps out ordinary MoneyTransaction rows carrying its id, and those rows are what every balance, every total and the category breakdown already derive from — so a generated entry moves them exactly as a typed one does, and can be edited or deleted like one. Generation runs on launch and on returning to the foreground, never on a timer and never in the background.

One rule, one day

The pair of sourceRuleID and the day an entry fell on is unique by a rule rather than a constraint, the way a category is unique by kind and name. The generator checks before writing, and StoreReconciler folds afterwards, because two devices that have not met yet can each record the same month's rent. A transaction with no sourceRuleID is never folded: two lunches on one day are two lunches.

Why categoryID is nullable

Deliberate: a half-finished category deletion must not destroy a transaction. The UI renders a missing category as Uncategorized. accountID gets the opposite treatment — a transaction with no account cannot move a balance, which is the module's whole purpose. Both ends of a transfer are required for the same reason.

Transfers sum to zero

AccountTransfer carries no kind and no category: the ordered pair of accounts is the direction. Across every account the net flow is zero, so moving money changes two balances and leaves total assets exactly where they were.

Debts do not sum to zero

A transfer's counterparty is another of the owner's accounts; a debt's lives outside the app, so DebtSummary.netFlow does not cancel out. Borrowing genuinely adds cash. Net worth therefore adds what is still owed to the owner and subtracts what the owner still owes, which is what keeps borrowing, lending and repaying from moving it at all. Only a debt naming no account moves it — correctly, because that money was spent before tracking began.

Projected interest is never counted

Debt.projectedInterest(asOf:) is shown on a card and reaches no total, exactly as SavingsDeposit.projectedInterest stays out of AssetSummary.netWorth. It is an estimate of what a rate implies, not a đồng anyone has handed over. Interest actually paid is an ordinary expense, because unlike the principal it really does leave the owner's money.

Why debtID is operationally required

The opposite call to categoryID, and for a concrete reason: the direction that signs a payment lives on its parent debt, so an orphan is not untidy, it is uncomputable. The stored UUID is optional only to satisfy CloudKit schema rules; drafts require a debt, summaries skip malformed orphans, and deleting a debt deletes its payments in the same save.

Why FundInstrument splits out

A price belongs to the instrument, not to the position. Before the split, nothing stopped two FundHolding rows carrying the same ticker at two different prices: net worth would have been wrong with nothing to show for it, and automatic refresh would have made it worse by fetching the same ticker twice. The catalogue now owns the price, and the columns the position used to carry it in are gone — so there is no second copy left for anything to disagree with.

Enums stored as String

CashAccountKind, TransactionKind, FundInstrumentKind and DebtDirection are all String, Codable. Raw values are stable across migrations and safe for CloudKit later.

currencyCode repeated across 9 models

Denormalised onto every money-bearing entity, always written from VNDCurrency.code. Nothing enforces that related records share a currency. The fund split removed one copy: a holding no longer stores it and reads the instrument's instead.

What flat FKs mean for iCloud

Helpful: CloudKit forbids unique attributes and requires optional relationships — neither exists here. Risky: manual joins do not self-heal, so a transaction that syncs before its account renders dangling until the account arrives.

Market-quote privacy boundary

Quote requests send a fund or ETF symbol, or a gold product type code, and nothing else — no balance, unit count, cost basis, or account name. A provider can learn which quote was requested, but not whether the owner holds it or how much.

One dependency, on purpose

Everything else is Apple's. A single pinned SwiftUI calendar view is the entire linked third-party package. Market access uses three small first-party URLSession providers behind one transport protocol; there is still no analytics or vendor SDK.

Import provenance, not ownership

A statement candidate's SHA-256 fingerprint is stored only after commit. Transactions carry one sourceImportID; transfers carry one per account side so a later statement from the other account can link the same transfer. These strings deduplicate imports but do not point back to the staged PDF, which is deleted after success.
